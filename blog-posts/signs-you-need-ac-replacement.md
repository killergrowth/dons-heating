Your air conditioner works hard every summer in Central Kansas — and like anything that works that hard for that long, it eventually hits a wall. The question isn't *if* your AC will need replacing. It's whether you'll catch the signs early enough to plan for it, or get blindsided by a full breakdown in the middle of a 100-degree August.

Here are the clearest signs you need AC replacement — and what to do when you spot them.

## Your System Is Over 15 Years Old

Age is the most straightforward indicator. Most central AC systems have a reliable lifespan of 15–20 years. If yours is pushing past that range, efficiency has likely dropped significantly — and repair costs are probably climbing.

Kansas summers put AC systems through serious stress. If your unit was installed in the early 2000s or before, it's worth getting a professional assessment rather than continuing to throw money at repairs.

## Repair Costs Are Adding Up Fast

A single repair is normal. Two or three in the same season? That's a different story.

A reliable rule of thumb: if the cost of a repair exceeds 50% of the cost of a new system, replacement is almost always the smarter financial move. Add up what you've spent on repairs over the past few years — that number tends to surprise homeowners.

If you're unsure whether repair or replacement makes more sense for your situation, [our air conditioning repair team](/air-conditioning-repair/) can walk you through an honest assessment.

## It's Blowing Warm or Inconsistent Air

An AC that can't hold a consistent temperature is one of the most common — and most annoying — signs of a failing system. If some rooms in your home feel like a freezer while others feel like a sauna, or if your unit is simply blowing air that never gets cold enough, the system may be losing its ability to function effectively.

This can stem from a failing compressor, low refrigerant, or aging components that no longer work together efficiently. Any of these issues in an older system typically point toward replacement rather than repair.

## Your Energy Bills Keep Climbing

If your utility bills have been creeping up year over year without a clear reason — no new appliances, no major lifestyle changes — your AC is likely working harder than it should to deliver the same results.

Older systems lose efficiency over time. A new, properly sized unit can reduce energy consumption significantly, often paying for a portion of the investment through lower monthly bills.

## It Uses R-22 Refrigerant

If your system was installed before 2010, there's a good chance it runs on R-22 refrigerant (Freon). R-22 has been phased out and is no longer produced in the U.S., which means the remaining supply is expensive and limited.

If your AC needs a refrigerant recharge and it uses R-22, that repair alone can cost more than it's worth. This is one situation where replacement isn't just practical — it's essentially unavoidable.

## Unusual Noises or Smells

Your AC should run relatively quietly. Grinding, banging, screeching, or rattling sounds are never normal — they typically signal failing motors, loose components, or compressor issues.

Burning or musty smells coming from your vents can indicate electrical problems or mold growth in the system. Neither should be ignored. If your system is older and producing these symptoms, replacement is often the cleanest solution.

## Frequent Cycling or Constant Running

An AC that short-cycles (turns on and off rapidly) or runs almost continuously without reaching your set temperature has a fundamental efficiency problem. This kind of strain accelerates wear on every component in the system.

If your system has been doing this for more than a season, you're burning energy and grinding down what's left of its working life.
