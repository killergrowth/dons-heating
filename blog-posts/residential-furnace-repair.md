## Common Furnace Problems in Kansas Homes

Kansas winters aren't always brutal, but when a cold front hits, your furnace needs to perform. These are the issues we see most often:

### 1. Furnace Blowing Cold Air
This usually points to a problem with the heat exchanger, a clogged filter, or a malfunctioning pilot light or ignitor. If warm air suddenly turns cold, check your filter first — a restricted airflow is one of the most common (and easiest to fix) culprits.

### 2. Furnace Short-Cycling
Short-cycling means your furnace fires up, runs briefly, then shuts off before the home reaches temperature. Causes include an overheating unit, a faulty flame sensor, or a thermostat issue. Left unaddressed, short-cycling adds serious wear and drives up your energy bill.

### 3. Furnace Not Turning On
If your furnace won't fire at all, start with the basics — check your thermostat settings, replace the battery, and confirm the circuit breaker hasn't tripped. If all of that checks out and it still won't start, you likely have an ignition or gas supply issue that needs a technician. Our detailed breakdown of [furnace not turning on causes](/blog/furnace-not-turning-on/) covers this step by step.

### 4. Strange Noises
- **Banging or popping** — often dirty burners or a delayed ignition
- **Squealing** — blower motor belt or bearing issue
- **Rattling** — loose panels or components
- **Rumbling** — could indicate a combustion issue — don't ignore this one

### 5. Pilot Light or Ignitor Failure
Older furnaces use a standing pilot light; newer units use electronic ignition. Either can fail. A yellow or flickering pilot flame (instead of steady blue) may indicate a carbon monoxide issue — get a technician involved immediately.

## DIY Checks Before You Call

Before scheduling a service visit, run through these quick checks:

1. **Thermostat** — Set to "Heat," temp set above current room temp, batteries fresh
2. **Air filter** — A clogged filter is the #1 preventable furnace problem; replace if it's visibly dirty
3. **Vents** — Make sure supply and return vents aren't blocked by furniture or rugs
4. **Circuit breaker** — Check the furnace breaker and the switch on the unit itself
5. **Gas supply** — Confirm your gas service is active and the shutoff valve near the furnace is open

If you've checked all of these and the furnace still isn't working correctly, it's time to call in a pro.

## When to Repair vs. Replace

Not every furnace problem justifies a full replacement. General guidance:

- **Repair** if the furnace is under 15 years old and the fix is under 50% of replacement cost
- **Replace** if it's 20+ years old, repairs are recurring, or efficiency has dropped significantly
