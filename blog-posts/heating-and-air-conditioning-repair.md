Your home's heating and cooling system works hard year-round — and when something goes wrong, you notice fast. Whether your furnace quits on a cold January night or your AC gives out in the middle of a Kansas summer, heating and air conditioning repair isn't something you can put off.

This guide walks you through the most common HVAC problems homeowners face, when to DIY versus when to call a pro, and what to expect when you schedule service with a local company you can trust.

## What "HVAC" Actually Covers

HVAC stands for heating, ventilation, and air conditioning. In most Central Kansas homes, that means:

- A furnace or heat pump for winter heating
- A central air conditioning system for summer cooling
- Ductwork and a thermostat connecting it all

When one part of that system breaks down, it often affects the others. A single failing component — like a blower motor or capacitor — can leave your whole home uncomfortable.

## Common Heating Repair Problems

Heating issues tend to show up suddenly, often when temperatures drop overnight. Here are the most frequent causes:

**Furnace won't turn on** — Usually a thermostat setting, a tripped circuit breaker, or a clogged filter cutting off airflow. If those check out fine, you likely have a deeper issue: a failed ignitor, a faulty limit switch, or a cracked heat exchanger.

**Weak or uneven heat** — If some rooms heat fine and others don't, check your vents and filters first. Persistent imbalance often points to duct leaks or a blower issue.

**Short cycling** — Your furnace fires up and shuts off before your home reaches temperature. This strains the system and usually indicates an overheating problem or failing component.

**Strange noises** — Banging, rattling, or squealing from your furnace are never normal. Each sound points to a different mechanical issue worth diagnosing before it becomes a breakdown.

## Common Air Conditioning Repair Problems

Kansas summers push AC systems hard. The most common issues we see:

**AC not cooling** — The system runs but can't keep up. This is often low refrigerant, a dirty condenser coil, or a failing compressor. If you're dealing with this, our [AC unit not cooling guide](/blog/ac-unit-not-cooling/) walks through every likely cause.

**AC won't turn on** — Check your thermostat and breaker first. If both are fine, a burned capacitor or failed contactor is usually responsible — both are common, both are repairable.

**Ice on the unit** — Ice forming on your indoor or outdoor unit signals a refrigerant or airflow problem. Shut the system down and call a tech. Running a frozen AC causes compressor damage.

**Water leaking indoors** — Your AC removes humidity from the air, which drains through a condensate line. When that line clogs, water backs up and can damage your ceiling or walls.

## When to Repair vs. When to Replace

This is the question we get most often. A few general rules:

- **Under 10 years old:** Repair almost always makes sense unless the damage is catastrophic.
- **10–15 years old:** Weigh the repair cost against replacement. A repair that's more than 50% of a new system's cost is usually a red flag.
- **Over 15 years old and failing frequently:** It's time to replace. Continuing to repair an aging system costs more in the long run.
