## What Does a 10 Ton Commercial HVAC Unit Actually Cost?

If you're managing a commercial property in El Dorado, Hillsboro, or Emporia — and you're staring down a failing HVAC system — cost is the first question on your mind. A 10 ton commercial HVAC unit is one of the most common sizes for mid-sized commercial spaces, and in 2026, the numbers have shifted enough that it's worth knowing exactly what you're getting into before you call anyone.

Here's the straight answer: a 10 ton commercial HVAC unit typically runs **$8,000–$15,000 for the equipment alone**, with total installed costs landing anywhere from **$12,000–$25,000** depending on unit type, installation complexity, and your specific building setup.

That's a wide range. Let's break it down.

---

## Equipment Cost by Unit Type

Not all 10 ton commercial units are built the same. The type of system matters a lot:

- **Rooftop Package Units (RTUs):** The most common choice for flat-roof commercial buildings. Equipment cost ranges from **$8,500–$14,000**. Installation is typically straightforward if existing ductwork is compatible.
- **Split System Commercial Units:** More flexibility for buildings without rooftop access. Equipment runs **$9,000–$15,000**, and installation labor tends to be higher.
- **Heat Pump Commercial Systems:** If you want heating and cooling from one unit, expect to pay a **10–20% premium** over a standard cooling-only system — but long-term energy savings can offset that.

Brand matters too. Systems from Rheem, Carrier, Trane, and Mitsubishi all fall in the commercial range — but their warranties, efficiency ratings, and service availability in Central Kansas vary. ## What Drives Installation Costs Up

Equipment is only part of the bill. Here's what adds to the total:

- **Crane rental for rooftop placement:** $500–$1,500 depending on building height and access
- **New or modified ductwork:** Can add $2,000–$6,000 if existing ducts don't match the new unit's airflow specs
- **Electrical upgrades:** 10 ton units draw significant power — if your panel or disconnect isn't rated, upgrades are required
- **Refrigerant line work:** For split systems, new line sets add material and labor
- **Permits and inspections:** Required in Kansas; typically $200–$600 depending on municipality

A straightforward rooftop replacement on a compatible system with minimal ductwork changes can come in under $15,000 total. A full system overhaul with new duct runs, electrical work, and crane service can push past $22,000.

## What Size Space Does a 10 Ton Unit Cover?

A 10 ton unit provides **120,000 BTUs of cooling capacity**, which is generally appropriate for **3,500–5,000 square feet** of commercial space — depending on insulation quality, ceiling height, sun exposure, and occupancy load.

Common applications in Central Kansas include:
- Small retail stores or strip mall units
- Medical or dental offices
- Restaurant dining rooms (not kitchens — those have additional heat loads)
- Light industrial spaces with moderate heat generation

Oversizing or undersizing a commercial unit is an expensive mistake. An oversized unit short-cycles, wears out faster, and struggles with humidity control. An undersized unit runs constantly and never quite keeps up. A proper load calculation — done on-site — is the only way to confirm 10 tons is the right fit for your building.

## What to Ask Before You Buy

Before you commit to any quote, get clarity on these:

1. **Is this a direct replacement or a new installation?** Replacements cost less if the infrastructure is compatible.
2. **What efficiency rating (EER/IEER) does the unit carry?** Higher efficiency costs more upfront but lowers monthly utility bills.
3. **What's the warranty?** Don's backs their commercial installations with a **10-year system warranty** — make sure you know what you're covered for.
4. **Does the quote include permits, crane, and startup?** Some contractors quote equipment and basic labor only. Get everything itemized.
