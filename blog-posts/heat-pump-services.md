# Heat Pump Services: The Complete Homeowner's Guide

If you've got a heat pump — or you're thinking about getting one — you probably already know they're not like a traditional furnace or air conditioner. They do double duty, handling both heating and cooling in one system. That's great for efficiency, but it also means when something goes wrong, or when it's time for maintenance, you need a tech who actually knows heat pumps.

This guide covers everything Central Kansas homeowners should know: how heat pumps work, what services they need, signs of trouble, and when to call a pro.

---

## How a Heat Pump Actually Works

A heat pump doesn't generate heat — it moves it. In summer, it pulls heat from inside your home and pushes it outside (just like an AC). In winter, it reverses direction and pulls heat from outdoor air into your home.

That reversal is handled by a component called the **reversing valve**, and it's one of the more common failure points. Understanding this helps explain why heat pump service isn't the same as standard AC or furnace work.

Most residential heat pumps in Kansas are **air-source systems**, meaning they exchange heat with outdoor air. You'll also see **mini-split heat pumps** (ductless systems) and **dual-fuel setups** that pair a heat pump with a gas furnace for backup on the coldest days.

## What's Included in Heat Pump Services

Heat pump services fall into a few categories:

### Routine Maintenance
An annual (or twice-yearly) tune-up is the single best thing you can do for your system. A proper heat pump maintenance visit typically includes:

- Checking refrigerant levels
- Inspecting and cleaning coils (indoor and outdoor)
- Testing the reversing valve
- Checking electrical connections and capacitors
- Inspecting the defrost cycle
- Lubricating moving parts
- Measuring airflow and static pressure

Most manufacturers require documented annual maintenance to keep warranties valid — including the **10-year system warranties** Don's backs on new installs.

### Heat Pump Repair
If your system is struggling, a repair visit diagnoses the issue and fixes it. Common heat pump repairs include:

- Refrigerant recharge or leak repair
- Reversing valve replacement
- Capacitor or contactor replacement
- Defrost board issues
- Blower motor or fan motor repair
- Thermostat or control board faults

## Signs Your Heat Pump Needs Service

Don't wait until your system dies in August. These are the signs to watch for:

- **Not heating or cooling adequately** — especially if it was fine last season
- **Running constantly** without reaching the set temperature
- **Ice buildup on the outdoor unit** outside of normal defrost cycles
- **Unusual noises** — grinding, squealing, or banging
- **Short cycling** — turning on and off rapidly
- **Higher energy bills** without a change in usage
- **Blowing warm air in cooling mode** (often a refrigerant or reversing valve issue)

Any of these is worth a call. Catching a small problem early almost always beats replacing a compressor later.
