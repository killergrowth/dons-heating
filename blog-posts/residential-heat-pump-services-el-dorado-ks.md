## What Is a Heat Pump and How Does It Work?

A heat pump is a single system that handles both heating and cooling. Instead of generating heat by burning fuel (like a furnace does), a heat pump moves heat from one place to another using refrigerant. In the summer, it pulls heat out of your home and dumps it outside. In the winter, it reverses the process — extracting heat from the outdoor air and pushing it inside.

Modern heat pumps are remarkably efficient, especially in climates like Central Kansas where temperatures don't often drop into extreme single digits for extended periods. That said, many homeowners pair a heat pump with a backup furnace — a setup called a dual-fuel or hybrid system — to cover the coldest nights.

## What Residential Heat Pump Services Include

"Heat pump services" isn't just one thing. Here's what falls under that umbrella:

### Installation

Installing a heat pump involves sizing the system for your home's square footage and insulation, selecting the right equipment, and running refrigerant lines, electrical connections, and ductwork as needed. Proper sizing is critical — an oversized unit short-cycles and wears out faster; an undersized one can't keep up.

### Routine Maintenance

Like any HVAC system, heat pumps need seasonal tune-ups. A standard maintenance visit includes:

* Cleaning coils and filters
* Checking refrigerant levels
* Inspecting electrical connections and capacitors
* Testing defrost cycles
* Verifying thermostat calibration

Skipping maintenance is the number one reason heat pumps fail prematurely. Annual checkups catch small issues before they turn into expensive repairs.

### Repairs

Heat pumps can develop a range of issues over time. Common repair needs include refrigerant leaks, failed capacitors, reversing valve problems (which cause the system to get stuck in heating or cooling mode), and defrost control failures. If your system is blowing lukewarm air, making unusual sounds, or running constantly without reaching your set temperature, it's time to call a technician.

### Replacement

Most heat pumps have a lifespan of 10–15 years with proper maintenance. If yours is aging out or repair costs are stacking up, a full replacement may be the more cost-effective path. A qualified contractor can help you evaluate that decision honestly.

## Signs Your Heat Pump Needs Attention

Don't wait for a full breakdown. Watch for these warning signs:

* **Reduced heating or cooling output** — rooms that used to be comfortable now feel off
* **Unusual noises** — grinding, squealing, or rattling during operation
* **Ice buildup** on the outdoor unit outside of normal defrost cycles
* **Higher utility bills** without a change in usage habits
* **System won't switch modes** — stuck heating when it should cool, or vice versa
* **Short cycling** — turning on and off more frequently than normal

Any of these symptoms warrant a professional inspection. Catching problems early almost always costs less than waiting.
