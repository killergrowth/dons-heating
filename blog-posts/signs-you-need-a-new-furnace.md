## How Do You Know When It's Time to Replace Your Furnace?

Most homeowners don't think about their furnace until something goes wrong. And when something does go wrong in the middle of a Central Kansas winter, the last thing you want to be doing is debating whether to repair or replace. Knowing the signs you need a new furnace ahead of time can save you from a cold house, a blown budget, and a lot of unnecessary stress.

At Don's Heating & Air, we've been keeping homes warm across El Dorado, Hillsboro, and Emporia since 1959. We've seen every furnace failure mode there is. Here's what we tell homeowners when they ask whether it's time to move on from their old unit.

---

## Warning Sign #1: Your Furnace Is Over 15–20 Years Old

Age is the single biggest predictor of furnace failure. Most furnaces are designed to last 15–20 years with regular maintenance. If yours is pushing that range — or past it — you're living on borrowed time. Even if it's still running, efficiency has likely dropped significantly, and the risk of a sudden breakdown climbs every year.

Don't wait for it to quit in January. A proactive replacement on your schedule is almost always cheaper than an emergency call during a cold snap.

---

## Warning Sign #2: Your Heating Bills Keep Climbing

If your energy bills have been creeping up year over year but your usage habits haven't changed, your furnace is losing efficiency. Older units work harder to produce the same amount of heat, burning more fuel in the process. A modern high-efficiency system can cut heating costs by 20–30% — savings that add up fast over a Kansas winter.

---

## Warning Sign #3: Frequent Repairs

One repair every few years is normal. Repairs every season? That's a red flag. When you're calling for service multiple times a year, the repair bills start stacking up fast — and you're still left with an aging system that's going to fail eventually anyway.

A good rule of thumb: if a single repair costs 50% or more of what a new unit would cost, replace it. You'll pay less and get a system that actually works reliably.

## Warning Sign #4: Uneven Heating or Cold Spots

Are some rooms in your house noticeably warmer than others? Is the living room comfortable while the bedrooms feel like a cave? Uneven heating often signals that your furnace can no longer distribute heat effectively across your home. It could be a ductwork issue — but in older systems, it's often the furnace itself losing capacity.

---

## Warning Sign #5: Strange Noises

A healthy furnace runs quietly in the background. If yours has started banging, popping, rattling, or screeching, those sounds aren't normal wear and tear — they're symptoms. Banging can indicate a cracked heat exchanger (a serious safety issue). Rattling may mean loose internal components. Any unusual noise is worth a professional look, and in older units, it often points toward replacement.

---

## Warning Sign #6: Yellow or Flickering Burner Flame

Your furnace burner flame should be steady and blue. If it's yellow, orange, or flickering, that's a sign of incomplete combustion — and potentially a carbon monoxide leak. This is a safety emergency, not a "wait and see." If you notice this, turn the system off and call immediately.

---

## Warning Sign #7: Excessive Dust or Dry Air

Older furnaces lose the ability to properly filter and humidify the air they circulate. If your home has gotten noticeably dustier, or your family is dealing with more dry skin, static electricity, or aggravated allergies, your aging furnace may be to blame. Modern systems paired with proper [indoor air quality](/indoor-air-quality/) equipment can make a significant difference in how your home feels.
