## First: Don't Panic — Check These Things First

Before you call anyone, run through these quick checks. Some furnace failures have simple fixes that don't require a technician at all.

### 1. Check Your Thermostat
Make sure it's set to **Heat** mode and the temperature is set above room temperature. Sounds obvious, but it's one of the most common causes of a "broken" furnace.

### 2. Check Your Filter
A severely clogged air filter can cause your furnace to shut down as a safety measure. If it's been more than 90 days since you last changed it, swap it out and see if the system restarts.

### 3. Check Your Circuit Breaker
Furnaces run on electricity even if they burn gas. Check your breaker panel for a tripped breaker labeled *Furnace* or *HVAC* and reset it if needed.

### 4. Check the Pilot Light or Error Code
Older furnaces have a pilot light that can blow out. Newer systems have diagnostic LED codes on the control board — check your owner's manual to decode them.

If none of that fixes it, it's time to call for emergency furnace repair.

## What Counts as a Furnace Emergency?

Not every furnace issue requires a middle-of-the-night call. Here's a quick breakdown:

**Call immediately:**
- No heat and outdoor temps below freezing
- You smell gas near your furnace (leave the house first, then call your gas company)
- Carbon monoxide detector is going off
- You have young children, elderly family members, or medical equipment in the home

**Can likely wait until morning:**
- Furnace is running but not keeping up with demand
- Unusual noise with the system still producing heat
- One room is significantly colder than others

When in doubt, call. A good HVAC company will give you an honest assessment of whether it's urgent.

## Common Causes of Sudden Furnace Failure

Most emergency calls come down to one of these issues:

- **Failed ignitor** — The most common furnace failure point; often replaced in under an hour
- **Dirty flame sensor** — Causes the furnace to start and immediately shut off
- **Tripped high-limit switch** — Usually triggered by restricted airflow (dirty filter, blocked vents)
- **Control board failure** — Less common but happens, especially on older systems
- **Gas valve issues** — Requires a licensed technician; do not attempt to diagnose yourself
