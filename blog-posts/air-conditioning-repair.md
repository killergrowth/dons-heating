## Signs Your Air Conditioner Needs Repair

Don't wait until the system fully quits. These are the warning signs most homeowners miss until it's too late:

- **Warm air blowing from vents** — If the air coming out isn't cold, you may have a refrigerant leak, a failing compressor, or a frozen evaporator coil.
- **Weak airflow** — Poor airflow usually points to a clogged filter, a failing blower motor, or ductwork problems.
- **Strange sounds** — Banging, rattling, or squealing are never normal. These sounds often signal loose or worn components that will get worse fast.
- **High humidity indoors** — A properly functioning AC removes moisture from the air. If your home feels muggy, the system isn't doing its job.
- **Frequent cycling** — If your AC is turning on and off every few minutes, something is off — whether it's a refrigerant issue, a dirty condenser, or a faulty thermostat.
- **Skyrocketing utility bills** — A sudden jump in your electric bill during cooling season often means the system is working harder than it should.

If you're seeing one or more of these, it's time to get a tech on-site before a small problem becomes a full replacement.

## Should You Repair or Replace?

This is the question every homeowner eventually faces. The general rule of thumb: if the repair costs more than 50% of the price of a new system and your unit is over 10 years old, replacement usually makes more financial sense.

Other factors that push toward replacement:

- Your system uses R-22 refrigerant (phased out — increasingly hard and expensive to source)
- You've had multiple repairs in the last 2–3 years
- Your energy bills keep climbing despite repairs
- The system can no longer keep the house comfortable on the hottest days

## How to Keep Your AC Running Longer

Repairs are inevitable, but preventive maintenance cuts down on frequency and cost:

- **Change your filter every 1–3 months** — A clogged filter is the #1 cause of avoidable AC problems.
- **Keep the outdoor unit clear** — Trim back any plants or debris within 2 feet of the condenser.
- **Schedule annual tune-ups** — A spring checkup before cooling season catches small issues before they become expensive failures.
- **Don't ignore small problems** — A weird noise today is a broken part next week.
