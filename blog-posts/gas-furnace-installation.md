## What Is Gas Furnace Installation?

Gas furnace installation is the process of removing your old heating system and replacing it with a new natural gas-powered unit — including all the electrical connections, gas line hookups, venting, and ductwork integration needed to make it run safely and efficiently.

For homeowners in El Dorado, Hillsboro, and Emporia, getting this right the first time matters. Kansas winters can be brutal, and a poorly installed furnace won't just cost you in comfort — it'll cost you in energy bills and repairs down the road.

Here's everything you need to know before you make the call.

---

## How Do You Know It's Time for a New Furnace?

Most gas furnaces last between 15 and 20 years. If yours is approaching that range, it's worth evaluating replacement even if it's still technically running. Common signs it's time:

- **Frequent repairs** — if you've called for service more than once in the past two years, replacement often costs less long-term
- **Rising energy bills** — older units lose efficiency as they age
- **Uneven heating** — some rooms hot, others cold
- **Yellow or flickering flame** — can indicate a cracked heat exchanger, which is a safety issue
- **Strange noises** — banging, rattling, or squealing

## Choosing the Right Gas Furnace

Not all furnaces are equal. Here's what to look at when selecting a new unit:

### AFUE Rating (Efficiency)

AFUE stands for Annual Fuel Utilization Efficiency. A furnace rated at 80% AFUE converts 80 cents of every dollar of gas into heat — the other 20% escapes as exhaust. High-efficiency models hit 96–98% AFUE and can significantly reduce your monthly gas bill, especially in a climate like Central Kansas where your furnace runs hard from October through March.

### Single-Stage vs. Two-Stage vs. Variable Speed

- **Single-stage:** On or off — simple and lower upfront cost
- **Two-stage:** High and low settings — more efficient, better comfort
- **Variable speed:** Adjusts output continuously — highest efficiency and most consistent temperatures

For most Kansas homes, a two-stage or variable-speed unit is worth the investment.

### Sizing

Undersized furnaces can't keep up on the coldest days. Oversized ones cycle on and off constantly, wasting fuel and shortening equipment life. Proper sizing requires a load calculation based on your home's square footage, insulation, windows, and layout — not a guess.

A reputable installer will always run this calculation before recommending a unit. If someone quotes you a furnace without asking about your home's specs, that's a red flag.

## What the Installation Process Looks Like

Here's a typical gas furnace installation from start to finish:

1. **In-home assessment** — Tech evaluates your current system, ductwork condition, and sizing needs
2. **Equipment selection** — You choose a unit based on efficiency goals and budget
3. **Old unit removal** — Existing furnace is safely disconnected and removed
4. **New unit placement** — Furnace is set in place and level
5. **Gas line connection** — Properly sized and leak-tested
6. **Venting installation** — Exhaust flue routed safely to the exterior
7. **Electrical hookup** — Thermostat wiring, blower connections
8. **Ductwork inspection and sealing** — Leaky ducts kill efficiency
9. **System startup and testing** — Full operational test before the tech leaves
10. **Homeowner walkthrough** — You learn how to operate and maintain the new system

A standard installation takes 4–8 hours depending on complexity.

## What Does Gas Furnace Installation Cost?

In Central Kansas, expect to pay somewhere in the range of **$2,500–$6,000** for a complete gas furnace installation, depending on:

- Unit size and efficiency rating
- Brand (Rheem, Carrier, Lennox, etc.)
- Complexity of the installation (new venting, ductwork modifications)
- Labor rates in your area

Higher-efficiency units cost more upfront but deliver lower monthly bills. Over a 10–15 year lifespan, the math often favors the premium model — especially with Kansas gas prices.
