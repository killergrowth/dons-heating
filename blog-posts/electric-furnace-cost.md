If you're pricing out an electric furnace for your El Dorado home, you've probably noticed the numbers all over the place — one website says $2,000, another says $8,000, and you're left wondering which one is lying to you. Neither is, really. They're just talking about different furnaces, different homes, and different installs.

Here's what electric furnace costs actually look like heading into 2027, and what makes your number land where it lands.

## What You'll Actually Pay

For most Central Kansas homes, a new electric furnace — unit plus professional installation — runs **$2,500 to $7,500**. That's a wide range, and it's wide for good reason: electric furnace pricing is driven almost entirely by two things — the size of the unit (measured in kW) and how much work it takes to get it installed correctly in your specific home.

A straightforward swap, same size, same location, minimal ductwork changes, tends to land on the lower end. A larger home needing a bigger unit, or a setup that requires electrical panel upgrades, new wiring, or duct modifications, pushes toward the higher end — sometimes beyond it.

## Why Electric Furnaces Cost What They Cost

**Unit size and capacity.** Electric furnaces are sized in kilowatts, and the right size depends on your home's square footage, insulation, and how cold it actually gets in your area. An undersized unit runs constantly and still can't keep up; an oversized one short-cycles and wears out faster. Getting this right isn't a guess — it's based on a proper load calculation, which is exactly why we [schedule an in-person assessment](/schedule/) before quoting anything.

**Electrical requirements.** This is the big one that catches people off guard. Electric furnaces draw a lot of power, and many homes — especially older ones in El Dorado's established neighborhoods — don't have the electrical capacity to support a larger unit without a panel upgrade. If your home needs new circuits, a bigger breaker panel, or additional wiring, that work gets added to your total.

**Ductwork condition.** If your existing ductwork is in good shape and properly sized, installation is faster and cheaper. If it's leaky, undersized, or poorly routed, you're looking at either duct sealing or a partial redesign — both of which add labor and materials to the job.

**Labor and local permitting.** Installation labor varies by the complexity of the job and whether electrical or structural work is involved. Permits are typically required for furnace replacements in most Kansas municipalities, and that's a cost that should always be included in your quote — if it's not mentioned, ask why.

## Electric vs. Gas: A Quick Cost Reality Check

A lot of homeowners comparing quotes are actually comparing electric furnaces against [gas furnace installation](/blog/gas-furnace-installation/) without realizing the cost structures work differently. Electric units generally have a lower upfront equipment cost than gas furnaces, but higher monthly operating costs in areas where electricity rates outpace natural gas — which is often the case here in Kansas.

If you're trying to decide between the two for your specific situation, our breakdown on [gas vs electric furnaces](/blog/gas-vs-electric-furnace/) walks through the full comparison, including long-term operating costs that matter more than the sticker price.

## Hidden Costs Homeowners Often Miss

A few line items that catch people off guard if they're not mentioned upfront:

- **Disposal of the old unit** — removing and properly disposing of your existing furnace
- **Thermostat compatibility** — older thermostats sometimes aren't compatible with newer electric furnace controls
- **Code compliance updates** — if your current setup doesn't meet current electrical or ventilation code, bringing it up to code is mandatory, not optional
- **Access difficulty** — furnaces in cramped crawl spaces, tight attics, or awkward closets take longer to install, which affects labor cost

A transparent quote should spell these out before you sign anything. If a quote seems unusually low compared to others you've gotten, it's worth asking directly whether these items are included.

## Financing and Making the Cost Work

Replacing a furnace isn't usually a purchase people plan for — it's often triggered by a breakdown in the middle of a Kansas winter. If cost is a concern, ask about financing options before you rule out a proper replacement in favor of a cheap patch job that won't last. We'd rather walk you through real numbers than have you make a decision in a panic with incomplete information.
