If your furnace is struggling to keep up with Central Kansas winters, you've probably started looking at replacements — and the first question most homeowners ask is the same: *how much is this going to cost?*

High efficiency furnaces carry a higher upfront price than standard units, but they also deliver lower monthly energy bills and longer system life. Here's a straightforward breakdown of what to expect in 2026 — so you can make a smart decision before the temperature drops.

## What Makes a Furnace "High Efficiency"?

Furnace efficiency is measured by AFUE — Annual Fuel Utilization Efficiency. It tells you what percentage of fuel actually becomes heat in your home.

- **Standard efficiency:** 80% AFUE — 20 cents of every dollar goes up the flue
- **High efficiency:** 90–98% AFUE — as little as 2–5 cents per dollar is wasted

In Kansas, where winter heating bills can run high and natural gas prices fluctuate, moving from an 80% to a 96% AFUE furnace can save $200–$400 per year on a typical home. Over a 10-year system life, that adds up fast.

## High Efficiency Furnace Cost Breakdown

Here's what homeowners in El Dorado, KS and across Central Kansas typically pay in 2026:

### Equipment Cost
- **90–93% AFUE furnace:** $900 – $1,600 (unit only)
- **95–98% AFUE furnace:** $1,200 – $2,500 (unit only)

Brands like Rheem — which Don's Heating & Air installs — fall in the mid-to-upper range and are known for reliability and warranty support.

### Installation Cost
Labor and installation typically run **$800 – $1,500**, depending on:
- Whether existing ductwork needs modification
- Venting changes (high efficiency units require PVC exhaust, not a standard flue)
- System size (measured in BTUs) based on your home's square footage

### Total Installed Cost
**Most homeowners in Central Kansas pay $1,800 – $4,000 installed** for a high efficiency furnace. Homes with complex ductwork, larger square footage, or code-required upgrades may run closer to $4,500.

## Factors That Affect Your Final Price

**Home size.** Bigger homes need higher BTU output, which means a larger — and more expensive — unit.

**Existing ductwork condition.** If your ducts are aging or undersized, they'll need work before a new system runs efficiently. Skipping this step defeats the purpose of buying a high efficiency unit.

**Venting requirements.** High efficiency furnaces use sealed combustion and exhaust through PVC pipes rather than a traditional chimney flue. If your home hasn't had a high efficiency unit before, this venting work adds to the install cost.

**Brand and warranty.** Premium brands with stronger warranty coverage cost more upfront but save money if something goes wrong. Don's Heating & Air backs installations with a [10-year system warranty](/furnace-installation/), which adds long-term value to the investment.

## Is a High Efficiency Furnace Worth It in Kansas?

Short answer: yes — for most homes.

If you're replacing an older 80% AFUE unit and plan to stay in your home for five or more years, the energy savings typically offset the higher upfront cost within 3–5 years. Kansas winters are real, and a furnace running at 96% efficiency is going to cost noticeably less to operate month over month.

## What About Rebates and Incentives?

Federal tax credits under the Inflation Reduction Act allow homeowners to claim up to **30% of the cost of qualifying high efficiency HVAC equipment**, up to $600 for furnaces. Kansas utility providers may also offer rebates for upgrading to high-efficiency systems. Ask your installer which equipment qualifies before you buy.
